# 🗺️ SCENE 2 — Long Travel Tutorial

Explain the travel system briefly before leaving.

### Tell the Players

> “Long-distance travel across the Sundered Isles will now use the world map. Each square represents roughly one day of travel on foot. Faster transportation can reduce that time.
> 
> Longer journeys require supplies and can cause exhaustion, and depending on the length of the journey, events may occur along the road.”

For this session, **do not introduce village side quests yet.**

They exist on the map/wiki, but today is just about learning the system.

### Tutorial Travel Event

Let the players believe the normal travel roll determines the event.

Keep the event short.

A few hours outside Solcrest, the party notices something strange:

> For several seconds, every bird along the road goes completely silent.
> 
> Somewhere beneath your feet comes a faint vibration.
> 
> Then it stops.
> 
> The birds begin singing again.

**DC 12 Arcana / Nature / Investigation**

**Success:**  
They notice faint purple crystal veins beneath exposed rock beside the road.

**DC 15:**  
The crystal seems to briefly pulse in the direction of Tulin.

**Failure:**  
Nothing obvious explains it.

Do not stop the journey. This is only a small reminder that something across the Isles has changed.

# 🔎 FINDING PASSAGE

Locals immediately know the obvious option.

### Asking Around

**DC 10 Persuasion / Investigation**

They learn:

> “Captain Rus Red is in port. Best captain in Tulin if you've got the coin.”

**DC 13**

They also learn he can usually be found at the tavern.

**DC 15**

Someone warns them:

> “Great sailor. Terrible person.”

Do not lock Rus Red behind a failed roll. Even on a failure, someone eventually points them toward the tavern.

# 🍺 SCENE 4 — Captain Rus Red

Rus Red is wealthy, immaculate, confident, and spectacularly condescending.

He looks at adventurers the way someone might inspect muddy boots.

### Approaching Rus Red

> Rus Red slowly looks over each of you.
> 
> “You require passage?”
> 
> He takes a drink.
> 
> “How unfortunate.”

If they mention Doge Town:

> “Doge Town? Certainly.”
> 
> “One thousand gold.”
> 
> He pauses.
> 
> “Each.”

If they complain:

> “You are not purchasing a chair on a ferry. You are purchasing passage aboard _my_ vessel.”
> 
> “If that number frightens you, I suspect the ocean will be considerably worse.”

If they attempt to negotiate:

**DC 18 Persuasion**

Rus Red dramatically reduces the price...

...to **900 gp each.**

He believes this is extraordinarily generous.

If threatened:

> “Wonderful. Now you're poor _and_ irritating.”

Nearby sailors immediately start paying attention.

Avoid combat here unless the players really force it.

# 🍻 SCENE 5 — The Drunk Pirate

At a nearby table sits the pirate.

Disheveled.

Half-drunk.

Possibly asleep.

An empty chair is beside him.

He overhears the conversation.

> “Oi.”
> 
> He raises one finger without looking up.
> 
> “Don't give that peacock a single copper.”

Rus Red rolls his eyes.

> “Do not listen to him.”

### His Offer

> “You lot need a ship?”
> 
> “I've got a ship.”
> 
> “You need somewhere to go?”
> 
> “Ships are famously good at that.”

Then he becomes unusually serious.

> “But somebody took something precious from me.”
> 
> “My parrot.”

The party eventually discovers the “parrot” is actually his **orange cat**.

If questioned:

> 
> “Orange.”
> 
> “Four legs.”
> 
> “Hates water.”
> 
> “Rare breed of parrot.”

### The Deal

> “Bring him back.”
> 
> “I'll take you wherever you're going.”

# 🐈 SCENE 6 — Finding the “Parrot”

Asking tavern patrons about the orange cat quickly reveals what happened.

### Investigation

**DC 10 Investigation / Persuasion**

Someone remembers the pirate gambling the cat during a card game.

**DC 12**

They learn the winner was a strange flumph gambler named **Lenny**.

**DC 14**

They learn Lenny is still playing cards nearby.

Important:

**Lenny did not steal the cat.**

The pirate drunkenly wagered him.

And lost.

Fairly.

---

# 🃏 SCENE 7 — Lenny the Flumph

The party eventually discovers Lenny floating beside a card table.

The orange cat is happily perched nearby or sitting on his head.

Lenny is perfectly polite.

### Lenny

> “Before anyone accuses me of anything, he wagered the cat.”
> 
> Lenny gestures toward the pirate.
> 
> “I specifically asked him three times whether he was certain.”
> 
> “He was.”

If they demand the cat:

> “I won him fairly.”
> 
> “But I would be willing to wager him.”

Lenny produces the cards.

> “Dead Man's Draw?”

---

# 🃏 DEAD MAN’S DRAW

Use the existing **Dead Man’s Draw player cheat sheet**.

Use the established six skill options for the party’s d20 rolls.

### Recommended Format

**Best of 3 rounds.**

Each round:

**Deal → Skill Rolls → Betting → Reveal**

Lenny plays **completely fairly**.

He doesn't need to cheat because he genuinely enjoys gambling.

### If the Party Wins

Lenny gently hands over the cat.

> “A deal is a deal.”
> 
> He looks at the cat.
> 
> “You could have done better.”

### If the Party Loses

Do not derail the session.

Lenny offers one final option:

> “Double or nothing?”

Give them one final sudden-death round.

If they somehow lose again, Lenny sighs and eventually lets them trade something silly, interesting, or sentimental for the cat.

The adventure continues.

# 💜 SCENE 9 — THE BUZZ

> The sand beneath your feet vibrates.
> 
> Not like an earthquake.
> 
> Like a humming string.
> 
> The vibration travels through your boots.
> 
> Your teeth.
> 
> Your bones.

### Everyone Makes:

**DC 13 Wisdom Saving Throw**

But this is not actually success vs failure.

It determines **what they experience.**

> The broken cove is gone.
>

> 
> Great formations of glowing crystal rise naturally from the cliffs and seafloor.

> There are no docks.
> 
> No ships.

>

Then—

SNAP.

They return.

# ⚔️ SCENE 10 — PREPARE THE BEACH

The party has only a brief moment before the attackers arrive.

Rather than literally running two minutes of combat time, give every player:

## TWO PREPARATION ACTIONS

They may do things such as:

- Extinguish fires.
    
- Take cover.
    
- Hide.
    
- Move crates into barricades.
    
- Reach high ground.
    
- Prepare a spell.
    
- Set a trap.
    
- Search for attackers.
    
- Protect the pirate.
    
- Move treasure.
    
- Ready ranged weapons.
    

Reward creative ideas.

Examples:

**Barricade:** Half cover.

**Good hiding location:** Advantage on first attack.

**Prepared spell:** Cast immediately when enemies land.

**Successful trap, DC 12 Survival:** First enemy entering it takes **1d6 damage** and falls prone.

**Extinguishing fires:** Prevents the ship from taking damage during combat.

# 🏰 Session 1 - Opening: Arrival in Solcrest

> **Purpose:** Introduce the characters, establish Solcrest as a lively city, meet Sir Pugsington and give the party time to explore before Princess Seleni's royal wedding.
# Phase 1 - Intros & Gate Scene

## 🎭 Character Arrivals

The characters arrive individually at the gates of **[[Solcrest]]**, joining an enormous crowd gathered for **[[Princess Seleni]]'s Royal Wedding**.

Describe briefly:
- Royal banners hanging from the walls with large sun symbols
- Huge crowds of travelers, merchants, peasants, and nobles
- Street vendors already working outside the gates
- Guards attempting to control the chaos
- Bells, music, carts blocking the road, animals, and festival noise

### Character Introductions

Ask each player:
**"What does the crowd see as your character approaches [[Solcrest]]?"**
Let each player briefly describe:
- Appearance
- Clothing / equipment
- How they approach the city
- General demeanor
Optionally give each character a tiny environmental interaction based on their personality/class.
## 👥 Getting Through the Crowd

The party must make their way through the packed crowd toward the gate.

Ask:
**"How do you make your way through?"**
Let players describe their approach before deciding the check.

### Possible Checks

|Approach|Check|
|---|---|
|Push through|Athletics|
|Slip through gaps|Acrobatics|
|Find the fastest route|Investigation|
|Watch the flow of people|Perception|
|Convince people to move|Persuasion|
|Pretend to be important|Deception|
|Scare people aside|Intimidation|
|Calm animals/carts blocking traffic|Animal Handling|

**Suggested DC:** 11-13

### Failure Complications

Failure should create a funny problem, **not stop progress**.

Possible complications:
- Drink/food spilled on them
- Child pesters them about their weapons
- Goat begins eating something they're carrying
- Pickpocket attempts to steal a few silver
- Sent briefly in the wrong direction
- Character collides with another player
Use collisions or shared problems to encourage the characters to naturally interact.
## 🚪 The Solcrest Gate
When they all finally reach the gate, say something like:

 “After fighting your way through the crowd, each of you eventually finds yourself pressed into the same small pocket of space near the front gate. Around you, strangers argue with guards, carts creak forward inches at a time, and somewhere behind you a goat is screaming like it has seen the end of the world.”
 
 “For the first time, the five of you are standing together.”

Then turn it over to them:

 “You’ve noticed each other at least a few times while moving through the crowd. Take a moment and tell me, what is the first thing your character notices about one of the others?”
  
Once the characters reach the entrance, an exhausted guard stops them.
The city has reached maximum capacity because of the wedding.

The guard steps forward:

“Next group!”

Pause.

Look at the players.

The guard assumes they are together.

“Names, purpose of visit, and proof of entry.”

Now the players have a tiny social dilemma:

**Do they correct him?**  
**Do they pretend they’re together?**  
**Does somebody naturally become spokesperson?**  
**Does one character say something ridiculous and drag everyone else into it?**

That’s where the party starts forming organically.

### The important trick

If somebody says:

“We’re not actually together.”

Have the guard barely care.

“Wonderful. You are now. I’ve got six hundred people behind you. Purpose of visit?”

The guard holds up a hand as one of the other guards comes and whispers in his ear, the city is now at occupancy for the wedding, no more people allowed into the city till after the wedding.
Unless someone is:
- A resident
- Royal staff
- A Performer
- Nobel
### Let the Players Try

Give them time to solve the problem themselves.

Possible approaches:
- Persuasion
- Deception
- Bribery
- Showing invitations
- Sneaking past
- Finding another entrance
- Name-dropping important people

### Useful Checks

**Persuasion DC 13**  
The guard sympathizes, but still cannot override the order.

**Insight DC 11**  
The guard isn't being cruel. He's exhausted and following orders.

**Perception DC 12**  
People carrying royal seals are being admitted immediately.

**Deception DC 14-15**  
Players pretending to be wedding staff, performers, nobles, etc. may have to prove it.

Allow creative attempts, but don't let failure derail the session.

## Then bring in Pugsington

I would let the gate conversation breathe for maybe **5-10 minutes**.

Let them fail.

Let the guard become increasingly apologetic but firm.

Then you hear:

> “That will be quite enough.”

Or even better, because Pugsington is tiny:

The guard suddenly looks downward.

Straightens.

Salutes.

> “Sir Pugsington.”

Then reveal him.

Pugsington looks over the five strangers currently arguing their case.

He asks:

> “You lot trying to attend the wedding?”

Someone says yes.

He looks at the guard.

> “They're with me.”

The guard pauses.

> “All five?”

Pugsington looks at them.

Maybe one of them is covered in festival drink. Another has a goat chewing on their cloak.

> “Apparently.”

And now Pugsington has accidentally **officialized the party**.

From that point forward, NPCs can casually refer to them as a group:

> “Your companions.”

> “Your friends.”

> “The rest of your party.”

And the characters can protest if they want.

That actually makes it funnier.
